Privacy, Data & Ethics

People first. We only analyse what leads to safer action.

We work in sensitive migration and protection settings, so how we collect, store, analyse and publish information is shaped by confidentiality and respect for survivors.

01

Data minimisation

Collect only what a defined prevention, protection or programme decision needs.

02

Purpose limitation

Do not reuse sensitive data merely because it is available.

03

Confidentiality

Limit access. Never disclose survivors’ identities, contacts or private locations.

04

Aggregated evidence

Publish trends, scam patterns and anonymous findings.

Do not publish unnecessarily

Sensitive details never belong in public communication.

  • Names or direct identifiers of survivors without informed consent.
  • Passport, national ID or immigration-document details.
  • Private addresses or exact locations that could create risk.
  • Medical or psychosocial details not needed for the purpose.
  • Case photos or stories used mainly for publicity or fundraising.
Our data promiseCollect less. Protect more.
  • Only the data a decision needs
  • Consent first
  • Restricted access
  • Totals and patterns, never names

Data lifecycle

Consent → Protect → Aggregate → Review → Act.

Safeguarding isn’t a last check before publishing. It starts before we ask the first question.

Public forms are not emergency reporting channels.

Please don’t send passport numbers, ID documents, exact private locations, medical records or detailed case information through public website forms.

Data confidentiality

We work with sensitive information about people at risk. We collect only what we need, keep it restricted to authorised people, and publish totals and patterns, never names. Surveys are anonymous, and no survivor data appears on this website.
